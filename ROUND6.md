# Round 6 — Planetary survival and environmental fidelity

This continues the existing game, Rapier world, numerical water solver, prediction, camera and WebSocket protocol. Tidefall and Pyrax are playable destinations. Cryon, a third hostile, advanced fabrication and volumetric weather are deferred.

1. **Disappearing rocks.** `InstancedMesh` cached aggregate bounds while instance matrices were still at their initial values. Uploading later placement matrices did not invalidate those bounds. The camera could consequently reject a batch whose visible instances were far from its cached volume. No directional chunk unloading or LOD switch caused this defect.

2. **Floating objects.** Rocks previously used an arbitrary center-height offset instead of transformed contact vertices. Plant geometry also started above its sampled root. Crystals used a fixed center offset despite variable height and rotation. Terrain rendering and physics already shared the same 2.5 m triangle mesh; that contract remains authoritative.

3. **Fix and evidence.** Rock placement now evaluates the transformed polyhedron against the exact triangular ground surface, with burial equal to 20% of rock size. The same vertices, scale and rotation build its convex collider. Crystals use transformed support geometry with 0.12 m plus 22% of their vertical scale buried; excessively steep crystal sites are suppressed. Vegetation starts at zero, is buried 0.025 m and tilts partway toward the surface normal. Instance and procedural geometry bounds are explicitly recomputed; plant bounds include wind displacement. F6 cycles bounds, three visible representative unculled wireframe copies, and off. It shows aggregate/geometry bounds, instance origins, a frozen camera frustum, visibility/culling state, fixed LOD and resident chunk ownership. Normal world culling remains enabled. Automated tests orbit representative formations on both worlds through 360°, three elevations and changed viewing directions; all placed rocks across four world seeds have the specified contact gap. The browser comparison was exercised. A native, freely moving mouse walkaround on both worlds remains manual QA; IAB rejects Pointer Lock. Earlier user confirmation covered Round 5 mouse look, not this full Round 6 acceptance sequence.

4. **Geology and rendering.** Existing coastal faults, drainage, cliffs and cave triangles remain. Pyrax adds a different warped basalt rift, escarpment, fractured ridges and thermal basin. World-space triplanar grain, surface-gradient bump detail, bedding, fine cracks, moisture darkening and roughness variation replace uniform smooth shading. Terrain colors vary by substrate/elevation, and sparse dry-world vegetation differs from coastal growth. This uses deterministic generated textures rather than downloaded assets. Small grain is shading detail, not thousands of colliders. `surfaceAt` exposes elevation, unit normal, material and region from the authoritative terrain query.

5. **Water, storms and tides.** Four finite-depth directional wind-wave components use `omega² = g k tanh(k h)`. CPU contact and generated GLSL use the same component table, phase and weather clock. Amplitude attenuates toward dry shores. Buoyancy, swimming contact, current drag and underwater camera detection include wave displacement; floating bodies also receive oscillatory water velocity and vertical drag. Numerical mean levels retain connected wetting/drying and boundary-volume accounting. Water adds Fresnel reflection, depth attenuation, shallow transparency, shore/crest foam, current streaks, sun glints, night shading and an offshore boundary extension. Tiny shader ripples are cosmetic. The mean solver remains 5 m / 20 Hz; the render grid is 2.5 m, with coarse distant water outside the playable domain. This is a linear wave approximation, not breaking-wave CFD or refraction around every boulder. Network means are quantized to centimetres; prediction/contact tests allow 1.5 cm.

   The existing lunar potential drives the 480-second semidiurnal cycle; its basin response factor is retained, not silently rescaled. Weather adds a separate smooth surge of up to 3 m, then a 4 m final-front contribution. The hydraulic boundary drives connected valley/cavern flooding and debris, rather than raising a decorative plane over isolated land. Storms build from 180–330 seconds, remain severe through 420, and dissipate by 500. Shared wind rises from 3 to 25 m/s, pressure anomaly reaches −28 hPa, visibility falls to 30%, and temperature, clouds and rain/ash change together. Wind affects flight, wave/current contact and vegetation. Predators avoid flooding and exposed high ground in severe weather. Rain/ash streaks and distant lightning are inexpensive effects; localized storm cells, volumetric clouds, ocean spray and delayed thunder are deferred.

6. **Day, night and astronomy.** Sun direction, daylight, fog, ambient/direct lighting, stars, the galactic band, moon terminator, water lighting and temperatures use server time. A camera-following suit spotlight preserves nearby navigation. Rotation and lunar motion now share the tide's accelerated physical clock (about 102 physical seconds per game second). Tidefall's modeled day lasts about 847 game seconds; night arrives during the round. Pyrax rotates faster. The moon uses the relative rotation/orbit rate; its meridian offset differs by pi, preserving the same quadrupolar phase. The inlet response retains its documented 45° lag. The 10° visual moon is still explicitly exaggerated from the approximately 1.63° physical disk. Stars/night lighting were captured; the moon was outside the captured level view, so there is no new Round 6 close-up moon screenshot.

7. **Two hostile variants.** The Tidal Stalker has 90 HP, quick pursuit, melee and visible ranged spores. The Ridgeback has 150 HP, a 1.35 m collision radius, 900 kg controller mass, slower pursuit, a larger plated silhouette, horns and exposed rear tissue. Armor takes 45% rifle damage; rear hits take 180%. Flanking requires five rear hits versus nineteen armored hits. Its 1.2-second charge telegraph commits to an attack direction, accelerates toward 8.5 m/s and delivers 24 damage plus bounded knockback once per charge. Lower tones and heavier steps distinguish its audio. Weapon feedback marks rear hits amber. These share the established AI/navigation, damage IDs, animation and collision pipeline.

8. **Encounters and combat.** Hit staggering has a 1.3-second recovery gate; ordinary repeated armor hits cannot silently cancel a charge. Rear hits can interrupt. Vision responds to darkness and storm visibility. Encounter checks run every 30 seconds after a 90-second opening, with a live budget of 3–6 scaled by crew and match phase, and a maximum of 12 total organisms. Terrain, tide, heat, world/habitat, darkness, weather and match progression influence placement/type. Candidates begin 32–57 m from a survivor; the final collision-safe location must still be hidden from every survivor, at least 28 m from everyone and within 70 m of the selected player. Validation precedes allocation, so rejected relocated habitats leave no actor/collider behind. Crowding and route quality still warrant longer human playtests.

9. **Resources and drops.** E collects oxygen, cells, liners, minerals, thermal compounds and medical tissue. The kit stores at most two medical organs and nine minerals. Medical treatment restores 32 HP; three minerals restore 65 jet energy and 50 rifle energy; thermal compounds reduce heat transfer for 60 seconds. Energy cells also restore 35 jet energy. Resources sit at expedition remains, cave/mineral sites and thermal terrain. Death creates exactly one server-owned dynamic anatomical drop: 650 kg/m³ biomass from a Stalker or 1250 kg/m³ chitin from a Ridgeback. Against 1030 kg/m³ water, these have different float/sink tendencies. Drops roll, receive buoyancy/drag, expire after 90 seconds and disable their physical body on pickup. IDs, idempotent death/drop state, capacity checks and authoritative ownership prevent duplicate rewards. Total objects are capped at 38.

10. **Jet and reticle.** Fuel now starts at 100, drains at 8 units/s and recharges at 9 units/s on the ground after a two-second thrust recovery delay: roughly 12.5 seconds of continuous thrust from full. Holding Space after jumping provides 16.5 m/s² thrust, with the existing acceleration, horizontal control, 16 m/s upward speed cap and stable capsule landing. Gravity and atmospheric wind affect the trajectory. Cells and mineral service supply field recharge. A 3 px dot with a restrained outline replaces the large reticle; firing, cooldown, interaction and hit feedback remain small. The rifle retains its authoritative camera-aligned ray, 18 base damage and 0.32-second cadence.

11. **Star map.** The host selects Tidefall or Pyrax from a layered orbital map in the lobby. Each has an actual terrain preview and shared gravity, stellar distance, atmosphere, ambient range, hazards and difficulty. Ambient ranges are evaluated from the same match weather function, not an unrelated label. Selection shows an orbital-descent loading panel. Client launch remains gated until its terrain/collision prediction world has initialized.

12. **World physics.** Tidefall uses 7.8 m/s² gravity, dense 1.2 kg/m³ air, the connected ocean, maritime storms and coastal chemistry. Pyrax uses 11.2 m/s², thin 0.55 kg/m³ air, no ocean/tides, ash precipitation, a faster rotation and dangerous thermal vents (over 200°C at the primary source). Gravity drives rigid bodies, falling, jump and jet trajectories, controller descent and buoyancy; atmospheric density scales airborne wind response. Stellar distance changes directional irradiance. A sealed suit is assumed to handle ambient atmosphere; underwater tank depletion remains the explicit respiratory resource. No atmospheric chemistry or volcanic-fluid solver is claimed. Signed seed namespaces identify the world consistently across terrain/physics consumers; snapshots also carry the explicit planet ID. Render state and prediction worlds are rebuilt on destination changes, preventing old-world actors, inventory or physics from carrying over.

13. **Multiplayer actually checked.** Two real browser clients joined one room, readied, loaded the same world and entered shared gameplay. Two independent socket tests cover host-only destination choice, matching world seed/water, inventory reset, wrong-world load rejection, launch gating, blocked mid-match transfer, creature targets/telegraphs/projectiles/damage/death, one shared anatomical drop, exclusive competing pickup, disconnect and rematch. Existing networking tests also retain survival, winner and reconnection coverage. Planet/weather/wave/astronomical phase derives from authoritative snapshot seed/time; decorative vertices are not networked. Browser captures used a separate local QA server on 4174; the user's 4173 room was not used as a fixture.

14. **Measured performance.** A 600-sample server benchmark after 120 warmup steps (a local fixture, not a production load test) measured total physics median/p95/max 0.44/1.63/2.89 ms without live creatures and 0.75/1.91/5.40 ms with three. Three-creature AI median/p95/max was 0.29/0.61/3.91 ms. In two simultaneous 1280×720 IAB views, the visible diagnostic sample was 35–36 FPS, 32–36 ms p95 frame time, 206–209 draw calls and 131k triangles. That includes development overhead and is not a native full-screen GPU benchmark. F3/F4/F6 retain timing, network, body, AI, weather and visibility diagnostics; normal gameplay hides them. The production Rapier module is still large (about 1.67 MB gzip); Vite reports its existing chunk-size warning.

16. **Remaining work by severity.** No known release-blocking bounds/collision/synchronization failure remained in the checked cases. Medium: native full-screen 360° walkaround and a complete natural 2–4 player match on each world are still required; IAB performance with two active WebGL views needs optimization; AI crowd routing and coarse shoreline/mean-grid transitions can be improved. Low: large physics download, dependency clock warning, approximate offshore waves, fixed weather timeline, simplified volcanic terrain and repeated expedition props. Third world/hostile, advanced fabrication and advanced atmospheric effects are deliberate scope deferrals. No public deployment or Docker runtime was exercised.

From `C:\Users\Kyler\Resolve`: `npm --prefix tidefall ci` for a fresh checkout, then `npm run tidefall`; open `http://localhost:4173/` in Edge/Chrome. Create/join, host chooses Star map, everyone readies, host starts. WASD/Shift move, mouse looks after capture, Space jumps/holds thrust, C dives, E collects, F uses the selected tool, Tab opens the kit, Escape releases capture/opens menu. Development F3 statistics, F4 collision/AI, F6 visibility. To check/build: `npm --prefix tidefall test`, `npm --prefix tidefall run typecheck`, `npm --prefix tidefall run build`. Production: `npm --prefix tidefall start`; use a persistent Node host with HTTPS and WebSocket upgrades to `/ws`, plus `HOST`/`PORT` as needed (defaults `::`/4173). `/health` reports readiness. Rooms remain single-process/in-memory. Docker recipe: `docker build -t tidefall ./tidefall`, then `docker run --rm -p 4173:4173 tidefall`; these are instructions, not a claim Docker was run.
