# Round 3 development and verification report

October 27, 2026 local session. Implemented in the existing Tidefall package, preserving repository history, numerical water, common collision geometry and authoritative WebSockets. Resolve source and its unrelated working changes are outside this checkpoint.

1. **Movement/camera causes.** Round 2 already repaired the original 10 Hz local snapshot motion versus independently smoothed camera. The remaining mouse-edge problem was its silent absolute-position drag fallback after Pointer Lock rejection. First-person magnified the importance of one presentation position and eliminating orbit-follow behavior. Investigation also found tiny contact settling at a spawn on the sloped landing-pad edge; all four balanced spawns now use the flat portion. No variable-delta replacement solver was introduced.

2. **Input and Pointer Lock.** The local camera is at eye height, with independent relative-delta yaw/pitch (±85°), zero roll, no local avatar and no third-person distance/follow controller. Clicking the viewport or Resume requests Pointer Lock through that gesture. Escape, focus loss, visibility changes and lock loss clear keys and pause input safely. Capture errors are visible and retryable. Desktop dragging and camera arrows were removed. Sensitivity and inversion remain. F is an accessibility alternative to item use; it does not substitute for mouse look.

   **Verification limit:** the embedded browser actually rejected capture, and this error path was verified without console errors. Native Edge control timed out waiting for app access. Actual native capture, cursor disappearance, physical mouse travel beyond the screen edge, ±85° mouse motion and native reacquisition therefore remain **unverified**. Pure lifecycle/delta tests pass; they are not a claim of native-browser certification. Use a full Edge/Chrome window for desktop play.

3. **Movement and physics.** Retained shared 60 Hz Rapier capsule prediction/replay, server acknowledgements, contact grounding, snap suppression on ascent, small steps, jump buffer and coyote time. Camera reads the same interpolated local position directly. Remote actors and props use buffered snapshots. Added remote capsule copies to prediction, monotonic input validation, explicit pause queue draining and bounded decaying shove velocity. Walking/swimming have separate speeds; currents are bounded. Emergency thrust has a finite 45-unit reserve without passive recharge. Terrain aiming is skipped unless the field is equipped.

4. **Celestial architecture.** Replaced the nearby moon at a fixed world coordinate with a camera-centered sky body, forced to background depth and drawn before terrestrial geometry. Its star-lit shader is independent of the planet's ambient lighting. A render-order grouping issue found during browser QA was corrected so the sky does not overdraw it. Physical diameter is calculated as `2 atan(Rmoon/distance)`; the displayed 10° disk is an explicit roughly sixfold enlargement of the approximately 1.63° physical configuration. Sky orbital motion uses real modeled seconds; the tide retains its separate time compression. A 300 m translation test gives zero sky parallax, and the actual cave roof visibly occludes the moon. Native free-look traversal remains bounded by the input-testing limit above.

5. **Survival rules and hazards.** Replaced core/beacon/extraction contracts with supplies, readiness, oxygen, thermal state, permanent elimination, spectators, standings and winners. Two to four players spawn separately with equal gear; solo practice is explicit and late arrivals spectate. Eight oxygen supplies, two liners and two batteries create scarce choices. Underwater oxygen drains before lethal damage. Heat transfer responds to water/air temperature, wind, immersion, insulation and shelter. Local vents, cold water, impact, stronger numerical sea boundary forcing, storm exposure and a late cold/pressure front supply the danger. There is no routine global health tax. The final signaled 9-minute fallback prevents stalls. Same-tick final deaths share first place; server ticks determine every duration.

6. **Multiplayer interactions.** Real capsules collide. A 2.8 m shove has a 1.25 s cooldown, bounded horizontal impulse and line-of-sight validation; wall blocking is tested. Field forces still affect objects and astronauts. Upright buoyant crates use matching cuboid collision/volume, and a canister follows a drifting crate. Supplies are claimed once on the server. Shelters provide a single protected bay, with shared occupancy and loss of protection when leaving. No weapons, crafting, hunger, base-building or NPC systems were added.

7. **Actual network/browser coverage.** Three independent browser tabs joined one room, readied, started and saw distinct astronauts and shared flooding. One delayed inbound messages by 80 ms. Actual E pickup stored a canister; the kit used it to restore oxygen. Actual field deployment consumed charge and displaced nearby astronauts. A real keyboard shove moved the target from z=-93 to approximately -92.23 while both remained at 100 health. Actual E fitted insulation and sealed a shelter. Refresh/HMR rejoined the same identities and match instead of creating duplicates.

   All three saw **Amber win**, with Violet eliminated by oxygen depletion and Ivory by hypothermia. Rematch reset readiness, oxygen, equipment and life state. On the next round, an astronaut died with the kit open: the repaired UI closed it, entered spectator mode while two remained, and cycled between both survivors. A later result correctly awarded the sheltered/insulated Ivory victory after Amber's cold exposure. Final captured browser error logs were empty; Fiber's known THREE.Clock deprecation remains.

   Browser hazard/view coverage used the isolated stdin QA runner to relocate players, advance water numerically and stage vulnerable oxygen/health values. Interactions and deaths still used real clients and normal server physics/exposure. These are **not** unassisted complete matches or WAN certification. The automated full storm test below does advance the entire match normally.

8. **UI simplification.** Removed the three-core checklist, mission stages, objective paragraphs, compass, persistent radar, extraction clock, field dashboard and visible development panel. Default play has a small health bar, relevant oxygen, tide/front status, survivor count, reticle and equipped-item label. Kit/status/chart live under Tab. Title offers Create, Join, How to Play and Settings; the lobby has the roster and ready/start. Notices expire; capture failure no longer occupies the center indefinitely. Death closes modal panels, results show ranks/times/causes, and rematch is explicit.

9. **World/immersion.** Preserved fault-controlled geology, drainage, sediment flats, shelves, volcanic source, cave, shared render/collision triangles and environment-conditioned vegetation. Added physical shelter shells and survival equipment, camera-centered sky, correct celestial occlusion, storm cloud/haze/lighting response, underwater low-pass audio, regional footsteps and oxygen warning tones. Water remains the connected conservative reduced-flow model, with numerical storm surge rather than disconnected plane flooding. Thermal coefficients, suit buoyancy, sky enlargement and optical/audio effects are documented approximations, not calibrated planetary predictions.

12. **Remaining issues, ranked.**

    * **High compatibility/testing limit:** the embedded preview cannot provide Pointer Lock and native Edge app access timed out. Full native mouse/capture testing is still required before claiming the primary desktop-control acceptance criterion fully verified.
    * **Medium:** multiple embedded canvases do not sustain 60 FPS here. Dynamic rigid-body/field contacts can require prediction corrections. The lazy WASM transfer is large. No independent WAN loss/jitter, four-player load or physical mobile validation was performed.
    * **Medium:** maps/shelter balance and complete unassisted human survival rounds need playtests. Shelters reduce thermal transfer; their geometry does not create a hydraulic air pocket. The sky's 10° disk is artistic. Fixed-step simulated timing is evidence for the implemented scenario, not broad balance certification.
    * **Medium hosting:** single-process memory rooms disappear on restart; shared routing, persistence and public-host validation are absent.
    * **Low:** THREE.Clock compatibility warning, simplified avatar/material art, upright crate and untransmitted rigid-body rotation approximations, coarse hydraulic grid and parametric thermal/chemistry/audio models remain.
